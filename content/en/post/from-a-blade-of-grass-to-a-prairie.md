*Original title: 从一株小草，到一片草原. Final-round speech, 16 April 2026. I received a Third Prize.*

Members of the judging panel, dear classmates, hello.

Today I want to begin with a story about my hometown.

My hometown is Ar Horqin Banner in Inner Mongolia. People there call it the "Grass Capital." Road signs often read, "Welcome to the Grass Capital." Visitors laugh and say, "Even the grass is welcoming us!" Every time I hear that, I have to hold back a smile as I explain, "I'm sorry, this character is pronounced *dū*, not *dōu*. 'Grass Capital' is short for 'capital of the grasslands,' not 'all the grass welcomes you.'" But if that is how everyone understands it, then perhaps our grass really is hospitable.

Later, I carried this joke with me across 1,800 kilometers, from the grasslands to Jiangnan. In this place where people cannot quite place my accent, I suddenly realized that some humor only reveals its weight after we leave home.

The joke has stayed with me because it contains an answer. Isn't seeking a dream exactly the process of growing from a blade of grass that the world welcomes into a prairie that can welcome the world?

"Seeking dreams across mountains and seas; the distance does not feel far." Those words describe us. So what does it mean to seek a dream? I want to offer an answer from my own experience.

## What are the mountains and seas?

For many people, mountains and seas mean geographical separation. For me, they are also a meeting between two cultures.

The grasslands of Inner Mongolia are the foundation of my life: broad, resilient, and shaped by the wisdom of following water and grass. The mountains and seas of Zhejiang have taught me to seek truth, to innovate, and to make every step solid. Those 1,800 kilometers are the whole journey of a young person moving from the familiar to the unfamiliar and from dependence to independence.

## What does seeking a dream mean?

Some say that seeking a dream is a grand voyage. But after arriving here, I discovered that it begins with the quiet persistence of ordinary days.

It is the persistence of the herders in my hometown, who spent fifteen years planting grass into land once covered by blowing sand, without knowing when the work would end. It is also my own early mornings and late nights at Zhejiang University, the moments I fell and rose again, and the gradual understanding of what that land taught me: grow quietly from a blade of grass into a prairie when no one else is watching.

That persistence is called putting down roots.

Seeking a dream is also about growing anew. We take the resilience and simplicity our hometowns gave us and weave them together with the reason and creativity the wider world teaches us. In doing so, we grow into someone new.

That process is called reshaping.

On the road toward a dream, one person's rootedness and reshaping will eventually flow into a wider land. Today, our generation stands at its own starting point. Seeking a dream is no longer only a journey away from home, nor only personal arrival and success. It means bringing the resilience and practicality that every step of our journey has taught us into the river of our time and the journey of national renewal. It means letting every ordinary day grow toward the horizon and every young person's strength shine where the country needs it. This is our generation's answer.

## Why does the distance not feel far?

Because when we are truly on the road toward a dream, setting out itself becomes the answer.

Is 1,800 kilometers far? Yes. But every step counts.

The people who fought desertification in my hometown spent fifteen years turning yellow sand into green. They did not feel the distance, because every blade of grass was bringing spring closer. We have traveled from the grasslands to Jiangnan, from study to practice, and we do not feel the distance either, because every today is bringing us closer to the tomorrow we want. Looking back, the mountains and seas are already behind us. Looking forward, the road still stretches far ahead.

The next time you visit Inner Mongolia and see "Welcome to the Grass Capital," you will know that it is my hometown. The grass there is more than a collection of plants. It is the resilience of generation after generation.

I set out from there and made my way here. Tomorrow, I will carry the foundation the grasslands gave me and the spirit Zhejiang University taught me toward farther mountains and seas.

Thank you.
