*Original title: 山海寻梦，不觉其远. Preliminary-round speech, 24 March 2026.*

Teachers and classmates, hello.

I want to begin with a joke about my hometown. It sits in Tianshan Town, Ar Horqin Banner, Chifeng City, Inner Mongolia. People there call it the "Grass Capital." Road signs read, "Welcome to the Grass Capital." Visitors often laugh and say, "Even the grass is welcoming us!" I carried that joke with me across 1,800 kilometers, from the grasslands to Jiangnan.

"Seeking dreams across mountains and seas; the distance does not feel far." Today I want to talk about what it means to seek a dream.

Seeking a dream begins with the courage to set out. Each of us has a grassland of our own. For some, it is home. For others, it is a comfort zone or everything we already know. The first step is to turn around and leave: to leave the place that says, "Welcome to the Grass Capital," and go somewhere where nobody knows us, a place where we must begin from zero.

That step is called courage.

Seeking a dream is also the quiet persistence of ordinary days. When I arrived here, I learned that it is not always a grand adventure. More often, it moves slowly, like a long stream. It is every early morning and every late night. It is every lecture we cannot follow, every assignment we cannot finish, and every paper we revise again and again. It is growing quietly from a blade of grass into a prairie when no one else is watching.

That persistence is called putting down roots.

Seeking a dream is also the courage to redefine yourself. We come from different places, with different accents, habits, and stories. At first, we may think that seeking a dream means becoming someone else. Later, we discover that it means becoming a better version of ourselves. We weave what our hometowns gave us together with what the wider world teaches us, and grow into someone new and more complete.

That process is called growth.

Why does the distance not feel far? Because once we are truly on the road toward a dream, mountains and seas stop being barriers. They become scenery. Is 1,800 kilometers far? Yes. But when our hearts are set on where we want to go, every step counts and every stage matters. When we look back, the mountains and seas are already behind us.

So the next time you visit Inner Mongolia and see "Welcome to the Grass Capital," you will know that it is my hometown. I have already set out from there and made my way here.

Thank you.
